# Affiliated Organisations — Flaticon Icon Recommendations

Curated for all **73 organisations** (55 अंगीकृत संघटना + 18 सलंग्न सेना)
rendered in the About-page sliders.

Each pick is driven by what the org **actually does**, not just keyword
matching. Where the literal translation of the name would lead to a generic
icon, the recommendation reaches for something culturally specific — e.g. the
Vanjari community were historically itinerant grain/salt traders, so the
suggested icon is a **bullock cart**, not a generic "community" glyph.

---

## How to install an icon

1. Click the search URL in the table → it opens a pre-filtered Flaticon search.
2. Pick the icon that best matches the **"Visual"** column.
3. Click **PNG → 128 × 128 → Free download**.
4. Rename to the exact slug shown in column 1 (e.g. `shetkari-sena.png`).
5. Drop the file into `public/icons/orgs/`.

The site auto-detects the new PNG — no code change needed. Until a file is
present for a given slug, a themed inline-SVG fallback renders in its place.

### Recommended Flaticon filters (apply once at the top of every search)

- **Style → Lineal** (line / outline icons, monochrome)
- **Colour → Black**
- **Size → 128 px** (sharp on retina, scales cleanly down to the 32 px badge)

Sticking to one style keeps all 73 icons visually coherent without you
having to match authors manually.

## Specific picks worth a word

A few choices that went beyond literal translation — these are the ones most
likely to give you a "ah, that's exactly right" reaction on screen:

- **`vanjari-samaj` → bullock cart.** The Vanjaris were historically India's
  itinerant salt and grain traders, moving caravans across the Deccan. A cart
  reads instantly as "trader on the move"; a generic "community" icon would
  lose that story.
- **`dakshin-bharat` / `tamil-samaj` / `kannada-samaj` → distinct temple
  styles.** Dravidian gopuram for South Indian, specifically Tamil-style
  gopuram for Tamil, Mysore palace for Kannada — each region gets a
  recognisably regional silhouette instead of a generic map.
- **`shiv-lahusena` → blood drop in a heart.** "Lahu" literally means blood;
  this is a blood-donation org, so a literal blood drop reads more clearly
  than abstract community/welfare.
- **`mali-samaj` → marigold.** The Mali caste are traditional flower growers
  (especially marigolds for temple offerings). The marigold is both their
  occupational identity and culturally instantly recognisable.
- **`jay-mh-mathadi` → porter carrying a head load.** Mathadi workers are a
  very specific class of Maharashtra-registered porters who carry loads on
  their heads at docks, markets, and warehouses. The pose tells the whole
  story.
- **`shivpratap-sena` → Maratha warrior helmet.** Named after Shivaji's
  "pratap" (valour). A Maratha-style war helmet anchors the heritage cleanly
  without needing a face/portrait.
